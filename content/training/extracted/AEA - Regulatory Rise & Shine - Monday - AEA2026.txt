RadAlt proposal
• §129.16 Radio Altimeter Systems
• (a) After [the initial RA performance deadline], no person may operate an
aircraft with 30 or more passenger seats or a payload capacity of more than
7,500 pounds under this part in the airspace of the 48 contiguous United
States and the District of Columbia with a radio altimeter unless the radio
altimeter system meets the performance requirements of §91.220(b) of this
chapter.
• (b) After [two years after the initial RA performance deadline], no person may
operate an aircraft under this part in the airspace of the 48 contiguous United
States and the District of Columbia with a radio altimeter unless the radio
altimeter system meets the performance requirements of §91.220(b) of this
chapter.

--- PAGE 7 ---
Timeline
• These regulations would require all aircraft equipped with radio
altimeters operating under part 121 and those aircraft with radio
altimeters operating under part 129 with 30 or more passenger seats
or a payload capacity of more than 7,500 pounds to comply with the
minimum performance requirements by the date the Federal
Communications Commission authorizes wireless services in the
Upper C-band.
• All other aircraft equipped with radio altimeters would be required to
comply with the same minimum performance requirements two
years later.

FAA Order 8300.16A CHG 1
• Purpose of This Order.
• This order provides guidance applicable to the data approval process
for major repairs and alterations, including field approvals. The source
basis for this order is Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (14
CFR) part 43.

--- PAGE 24 ---
FAA Order 8300.16A CHG 1
• Audience.
• The audience for this order is responsible Flight Standards offices, aviation
safety inspectors (ASI), Aircraft Certification Offices (ACO), Aviation Safety
Engineers (ASE),designees (Designated Engineering Representatives (DER),
Organization Designation Authorizations (ODA), and Designated
Airworthiness Representatives (DAR)), and others engaged in the approval
process for major repairs, major alterations, and the data associated with
each. For the purpose of this order, the use of the term “ASI” includes DARs
authorized to approve data in support of major repairs or alterations when
performing work in accordance with Federal Aviation Administration (FAA)
Order 8000.95, Designee Management Policy.

--- PAGE 25 ---
FAA Order 8300.16A CHG 1
• SUBJ: Major Repair and Alteration Data Approval
• This order provides guidance on the responsibilities and requirements
for the approval of technical data associated with major repairs or
alterations. Types and sources of technical data are discussed.
Considerations that must be addressed in the development of
technical data to substantiate major repairs or alterations are also
addressed.
• This order does not provide guidance for making decisions on major or
minor changes in type design.

--- PAGE 26 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• The following lists contain method(s) that may be used for
approval of data supporting major alterations to type
certificated (TC) and Supplemental Type Certificated (STC)
products. These lists are not all-inclusive, and each alteration
should be evaluated on a case-by-case basis. Consult each
section that concerns the aeronautical product. Additionally,
aviation safety inspectors (ASI) should review current notices,
advisory circulars (AC), etc., for specific types of installations
that have been identified as candidates for field data approval.
The legend is as follows:

--- PAGE 27 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• 1. STC. Items with the letters “STC” identify changes that are
determined to be major changes to type design (ref 14 CFR 21.93)
and require an STC.
• Taking into consideration the complexity of a broad range of
applications concerning major alterations, inspectors occasionally
encounter a situation in which the guidance material identifies
“STC”, but the applicant feels the change doesn't warrant approval
as a major change to the product's type design.
• The applicant may request a reclassification for the specific
alteration. See paragraph 4. “Reclassification Process”.

--- PAGE 28 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• 2. EVL. Data supporting items designated ‘Evaluation” by the
letters “EVL” may be eligible for approval by means other than a
STC, depending on the scope and complexity of the alteration.
• These items will not automatically qualify for a field approval;
they require evaluation and review of guidance to determine if
the field approval process may be applied and is appropriate.
• The ASI may seek assistance from the appropriate ACB if
necessary, in making determinations of items listed as “EVL”.

--- PAGE 29 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• 3. ENG. Data supporting items designated “Engineering" by the
letters “ENG” may be eligible for approval by means other than a
STC, but may not qualify for field approval.
• This data may be obtained from a Designated Engineering
Representative (DER), Organization Designation Authorization
(ODA) approved engineering data, or through an ACB
coordinated field approval.

--- PAGE 30 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• 4. Reclassification Process. If the legend indicates an STC is
required, but the applicant believes that the change does not
warrant an STC, the applicant may request a reclassification for
the specific alteration.
• Likewise, if the legend indicates EVL or ENG, but the FAA
determines that the alteration/repair constitutes a major change
to type design and therefore requires an STC, the applicant will
be notified of that determination but still may request a
reclassification for the specific alteration.

--- PAGE 31 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• a. The applicant will forward the request for reclassification to
the local Flight Standards District Office (FSDO). The FSDO
should ensure that a complete request includes at a minimum:
• (1) A description of the alteration to include information justifying the
reclassification and identification of the product it is being installed on.
• (2) The correct identification of the section in the Major
Repair/Alteration Job Aid against which a reclassification is being
requested, including the revision status of the job aid.

--- PAGE 32 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• b. Each FSDO will evaluate the request based on regulatory
requirements including a collaborative decision that involves the ASI,
office management, and the associated Aircraft Certification Service
(AIR) office.
• (1) If the FSDO does not concur, they will notify the applicant with an
explanation why the reclassification request was inappropriate. The applicant
may request the FSDO review of a non-concur using FAA Consistency and
Standardization Initiative (CSI) procedures.
• (2) If the FSDO supports the reclassification and believes further
consideration is warranted, the FSDO will send their concurrence, along with
the request to the appropriate Aircraft Certification Branch preferably by
electronic communication.

--- PAGE 33 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
• c. The ACB will evaluate the request from an engineering standpoint using
an interdependent process involving the FSDO ASI, FSDO Management.
AIR should be used to collaboratively reach a decision on the
reclassification appeal request.
• (1) If the ACB does not concur with the reclassification request, the ACB will notify the
FSDO with an explanation as to why the reclassification request was inappropriate.
The FSDO will forward this to the applicant.
• (2) The ACB will then return the reclassification request with the Approval or Denial to
the FSDO.
• (3) The FSDO will then inform the applicant that the repair or alteration does, or does
not require a STC, as applicable, and will evaluate the package accordingly.

--- PAGE 34 ---
ELIGIBILITY CONSIDERATIONS FOR FIELD DATA APPROVAL
NOTE:
• The FSDO should ensure that the applicant has referenced the
concurrence on the FAA Form 337 prior to issuing the field data
approval or documenting the alteration with approved data.
