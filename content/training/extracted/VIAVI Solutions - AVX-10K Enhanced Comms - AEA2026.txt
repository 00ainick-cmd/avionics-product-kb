Direct Connection Testing
 Done with radio connected via
coax directly to the AVX-10K.
 Can aid in troubleshooting to
determine if it is a bad LRU or
if the Coax/antenna is not
functioning as needed.

should be take not to transmit
for to long.

signals
̶ Transmissions in direct connect
mode should be limited to short
bursts as to not overload the unit

Over the Air Test Plans
 First the radio being tested needs to have
had its output powers measured recently via
some calibration or watt meter measurement
to find that it is still within the manufacture's
specifications.
 Doing a simply transmission test with a
watt meter in line can obtain the values
needed to verify against the manufacture’s
specification.
 You can also use the direct connection
measurement with the AVX-10K.
 Once we are sure that the Comms system
is fully functional, we can set the over the air
reference points.

Over the Air Test Plans
 Position the AVX-10K near the UUT antenna with a direct line of
sight, and the telescoping antenna fully extended.
 Then find a location that the AVX-10K can receive and transmit
to the aircraft. A received power level of 0dBm should be
sufficient.
 Record the following in reference to Comms antenna:
̶ Frequency of test
̶ Distance
̶ Height
̶ Angle
̶ Received Power
 When testing other aircraft with the same equipment these
values can be used for reference verification.

Over the Air Test Plans
 The receiver sensitivity tests for Comms is another important test.
 Using the AVX-10K the RF level can be set and transmitted to the aircraft radio.
 If you account for the transmission power and the over the air loss, then the sensitivity of the radio can be
found.
 Using the Two-Way voice communications Technician A can slowly increase the power of the AVX-10K
while communicating with Technician B in the aircraft to verify the sensitivity is within passing ranges.
AVX-10K + Path Loss = Sensitivity (must be -73 dBm or smaller)
-44 dBm + (-30 dB) = -74 dBm = PASS

Over the Air Test Plans
Aircraft Comms Channel spacing
• Older radios that only meet the standard for
25kHz spacing had wider tolerances for
accuracy but with spacing of 8.33kHz they need
to be much more accurate.
• To test this you would simply slowly step the
frequency transmitting from the AVX-10K to the
aircraft to verify it does not let signals through.
• To verify the frequency accuracy from the
aircraft you simple need to reference the
frequency error meter.
